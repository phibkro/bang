## Tasks

### T01 — Orient

From the permitted surface, explain in your own words what BANG is trying to make possible and locate
the smallest example that combines a semantic contract with a local resource obligation.

### T02 — Establish the example's evidence

Use the product to establish the example's observable result, whether its alternative realizations meet
their shared law, and whether a machine consumer can retrieve one joined description of contracts,
realizations, resource obligations, laws, and compiler evidence. Preserve the evidence.

### T03 — Make and recover from a realistic mistake

In your disposable workspace, change the example so the permit is used twice where it was promised once.
Capture the product's diagnosis. Use only public help to recover while keeping both uses, then prove the
revised program is accepted. Explain what the diagnosis taught you.

### T04 — Transfer the semantic model

Change which provided realization is installed without changing the shared computation. Before running,
predict the observable result and whether the shared law should still hold. Run the relevant checks and
compare evidence from before and after the swap.

### T05 — Inspect a concrete compilation consequence

Find concrete emitted evidence for what happens to the value declared unused in the example. Determine
whether its binding expression still runs and whether a runtime environment cell is retained. Cite the
artifact evidence rather than repeating documentation.

### T06 — Judge machine-readable invalidity

Ask the product for its joined machine-readable contract view of the invalid twice-used version from T03.
Decide from the actual response whether a naive automation consumer could mistake the operation's success
for program validity. Identify the safest validity signal in the response.
